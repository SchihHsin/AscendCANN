from importing an existing site to shipping pages, redesigning a brand, running

The slurp: getting your site into Ploy
The demo opened where a new user opens — onboarding. Ploy reads the domain tied
to your email and kicks off a site import the team calls the slurp. Q, the
engineer behind it, explained the idea behind its codename, Rosetta:
“
Given any website on the internet, its bare components are HTML and CSS. How can we translate that into something Ploy can work with?
”
Q
Engineering, Ploy

was candid that most of it deliberately isn’t AI, because no model reliably
handles every CSS rule, responsive breakpoint, and hover state. The honest
framing everyone appreciated:
“
A site slurp is not meant to be a one-to-one clone. It&#x27;s best effort — but best-in-class — at codifying your existing site into a design system you can actually build on.
”

point on why that matters:
“
You get true production-quality code, as if a developer wrote it. It&#x27;s easier for agents to work with — but it&#x27;s also easier for humans to work with.
”

Co-founder &amp; CEO, Ploy

Ploy. Will pushed on the timeline:

Ad creatives as code
The moment that got the biggest reaction: building an ad creative as code.
Because a creative is a real page, it renders deterministically — no
uncanny-valley artifacts, no extra fingers — and every element becomes a prop, so
you can generate variations of copy, layout, and background instantly. Bryant put
the claim plainly:
“
This is probably the only product out there that can build an ad creative as if a visual designer built it.
”
Bryant Chou
Co-founder &amp; CEO, Ploy

Q&amp;A: the questions worth replaying
The last twenty minutes were live questions, and Bryant’s answers were some of
the most useful parts of the session. Here’s each one, and where to find it in
the replay.
Do you have to run your whole site on Ploy, or can you embed it alongside an existing CMS?
You don’t have to move everything. Ploy’s built-in reverse proxy serves your
Ploy-built pages while every other page keeps running on your existing CMS —
Webflow, WordPress, whatever you’re on. Several customers already run exactly
this way, and if you later want to migrate the rest, Ploy can help you do it.
(Bryant also teased a native Ploy CMS — a flat-file version exists today, with a
richer one on the way.)
Watch at 40:00 →
Does Ploy handle site maintenance automatically — SSL, broken links, caching, image compression?
You skip the onerous upkeep of a typical WordPress site. Ploy doesn’t silently
change things for you; instead it surfaces suggestions — a missing tracking
pixel, a traffic dip, a content gap — and you click run to fix them. Connect
Google Analytics, Search Console, and a rank tracker, and the proactive system
keeps working on the site between logins. As Bryant put it, it’s the whole
website working while you sleep.
Watch at 41:16 →
When I publish, do changes go to my live homepage or to a new redirected page?
The cleanest path is to point your DNS at Ploy and set up the reverse proxy, so
the requests you don’t want Ploy to serve are routed accordingly. It’s a bit
technical, but it’s documented — and you can adopt Ploy piecemeal, page by page,
rather than in one big cutover.
Watch at 43:04 →
I haven’t done anything with SEO or AEO yet — where should I start?
Just sign up and see what Ploy recommends. The models are capable enough now that
anyone can start from wherever they are. Bryant flagged a big Lookbook update for
redesigning from scratch, plus PloyBooks to help migrate an existing site over.
Watch at 44:13 →
What do I give up by not hosting everything on Ploy? Is the goal to migrate it all?
Many customers start small and migrate over time — one moved a custom Next.js
site across in a single afternoon, agentically. Hosting with Ploy is what unlocks
the flywheels: client-side events, analytics, and visitor enrichment — seeing
which companies visit, then triggering a PloyBook that researches the account and
emails the right person. Skip hosting and you skip those loops.
Watch at 45:18 →
How does analytics and event instrumentation work — do I add Google Tag Manager?
You can bring your existing GTM, but Ploy also ships analytics out of the box,
auto-instrumented — sessions, pageviews, scroll depth, and clicks, all tied to an
anonymous ID. That matters for ad tracking: you get the raw client- and
server-side data to build a true picture of where spend goes, and you can ask
Ploy to compile a report, drop it in Slack, or email it daily.
Watch at 47:04 →
Where does Ploy get its copy and design best practices — my brand, or its own strategies?
Both. Out of the box you get the team’s copywriting and web-design skills, held
to Ploy’s own bar — its taste, imbued in the product. Then you shape it with
workspace memory: tell Ploy your preferences once and it codifies them into a
document added to every context window, so they’re remembered going forward.
Watch at 48:25 →
What integrations does Ploy have — including CRM?
A lot. Design through Figma (how agencies bring a brand’s fonts and assets in),
analytics tools, CRM connections with Attio, HubSpot, and Salesforce, spreadsheets
and the rest of G Suite, Zoom for meeting recordings, LinkedIn and X for posting.
Two things that surprised the room: you can put PloyBooks on a schedule (one
community member runs a business-development PloyBook every morning), and
webhooks can trigger PloyBooks programmatically. And because everything in Ploy
is multiplayer, you can invite teammates into the same chat.
Watch at 50:04 →
Won’t Google penalize AI slop? How do you handle that?
It’s built into Ploy’s skills. There’s an anti-slop skill that catches the usual
AI tells — em-dash overuse, the self-contradictory “it’s not this, it’s that”
copy pattern — and on the design side, the entire thesis behind the Lookbook is
building something bespoke rather than the same out-of-the-box template.
Watch at 53:18 →
What kind of results have people been seeing?
On the organic side, Ploy’s own site went from barely ranking to broadly indexed,
with a real surge in interest as pages got picked up. On paid, teams have seen
meaningful lifts in landing-page conversions when the page is genuinely optimized
for the ad behind it.
Watch at 54:22 →
