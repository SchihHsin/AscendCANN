Privacy & compliance
Built on consent, not scraping.
Ploy resolves visitors only when they&#x27;re U.S.-located and present in an opted-in partner network where consumers explicitly consented to third-party marketing. Every identified contact carries a verifiable consent record — CAN-SPAM compliant, SOC 2 Type II, Spamhaus-aligned, CCPA opt-out honored. Your job as a Ploy customer is to keep three things in place.
Opt-out
Opt-out link in every email
Every marketing email you send to an identified contact needs a clear, working unsubscribe link. CAN-SPAM requires it; Spamhaus enforces it.
Disclosure
Privacy policy disclosure
Update your site privacy policy to disclose that you use a third-party identity graph to recognize business visitors. Ploy provides recommended language you can paste in.
Consent
Cookie banner enforcement
Wire your cookie banner so visitors who decline are excluded from identification. Ploy honors the Do Not Track signal at the resolution layer once the banner is connected.
Privacy FAQ
Visitor identification, answered.
Is Ploy&#x27;s visitor identification compliant with privacy regulations?
Yes. Privacy and compliance are built into Ploy&#x27;s visitor identification from day one, with multiple safeguards so that identity resolution only proceeds when it is legally permissible.
CAN-SPAM:
All identity data comes from an opted-in partner network. Verifiable consent records — timestamp, source, and IP — are maintained for every contact, aligned with Spamhaus best practices to avoid spam traps.
CCPA:
California residents can opt out of the sale of their personal information through a dedicated opt-out form. Ploy customers can also flag opt-out requests directly through the established Do Not Sell process.
GDPR:
Ploy does not collect or share net-new anonymous visitor data in GDPR-regulated regions such as the EU and UK. If your brand already has an existing relationship with a user — for example someone from Canada or the EU who opted in on your site — that identity can be resolved, but only when the relationship already exists.
Security:
Ploy maintains industry-standard compliance with SOC 2 Type II controls. Audit reports and certifications are available on request for Enterprise customers.

Where does the identity data come from?
From an opted-in partner network of websites where consumers have agreed to share their information for third-party marketing. When one of those consented individuals visits your site, the underlying identity graph resolves the visit to a person and company. No data is purchased from scraped sources, no LinkedIn fingerprinting is performed, and no SDK is installed on visitor devices to capture identifying information.
Does Ploy identify visitors outside the United States?
For net-new anonymous visitors, no — identities are only resolved when the visitor is located in the United States, enforced through geofencing on the resolution endpoint. The exception is when your brand already has an existing relationship with the visitor (e.g. they previously opted in on your site); in that case the identity can be resolved regardless of location.
What about visitors who decline cookies or enable Do Not Track?
When a visitor declines cookies on your banner — or signals Do Not Track / Global Privacy Control — Ploy suppresses identification for that visitor. The signal is honored at the resolution layer, not just on the cookie itself, so a declined visitor is never resolved to a person even if they would otherwise match the opted-in network. Your cookie banner is the gate.
How do consumers opt out?
Identified consumers can exercise their CCPA rights — including the right to opt out of sale or sharing and the right to deletion — through a public opt-out form maintained by the data provider. Once a consumer opts out, their identity is removed from the network and will not be resolved on any downstream site, including yours. Ploy passes through CCPA requests automatically; you don&#x27;t need to handle them yourself.
What should you look for in B2B website visitor identification software?
Look for five things: company and person-level resolution for the traffic you are eligible to identify, useful firmographic enrichment, intent scoring from real page behavior, direct CRM routing, and clear consent and geographic controls. The best fit is the platform that turns an eligible anonymous visit into an account your team can evaluate and work without a manual export.
Can Ploy identify people or only companies?
Ploy can resolve eligible U.S. traffic to a company and likely contacts when the visitor matches its opted-in partner network. Each matched account can include firmographics, headcount, industry, funding stage, and likely contacts with LinkedIn profiles. When a person-level match is not available, the company-level visit and its on-site behavior can still be used for account scoring.
What are my responsibilities as a Ploy customer?
Three things. (1) Include a clear, working opt-out link in every marketing email you send to identified contacts — CAN-SPAM requires it and Spamhaus enforces it. (2) Update your website privacy policy with language disclosing that you use a third-party identity graph to recognize business visitors; Ploy provides recommended language you can paste in. (3) Configure your cookie banner so visitors who decline cookies are excluded from identification — Ploy&#x27;s Do Not Track integration handles the enforcement once the signal is wired up.
